Bear Bobcat
Required
Official adventure page ↗

The den's first adventure of the year: getting to know each other, learning the Scout Oath and Law, and setting a den Code of Conduct together. It fills the whole Sept 10 den meeting.

  • Get to know members of your den.
  • Recite the Scout Oath and Law with your den and den leader.
  • Learn about the Scout Oath. Identify the three points of the Scout Oath.
  • With your den, create a den Code of Conduct.
  • Learn about the denner position and responsibilities.
  • Demonstrate the Cub Scout sign, Cub Scout salute, and Cub Scout handshake. Show how each is used.
  • Share with your den, or family, a time when you demonstrated the Cub Scout motto “Do Your Best.” Explain why it is important to do your best.
  • At home, with your parent or legal guardian, do the activities in the booklet How to Protect Your Children From Child Abuse: A Parent's Guide.Home

Bear Strong
Required
Official adventure page ↗

A full den meeting for the food tasting, active games, and relaxation activity, with room to enjoy each one instead of rushing through.

  • Sample food from 3 of the following food groups: protein, vegetables, fruits, dairy, and grains.
  • Be active for 30 minutes with your den or at least one other person, including both stretching and moving.
  • Be active for 15 minutes doing personal exercises that boost your heart rate, use your muscles, and work on flexibility.
  • Do a relaxing activity for 10 minutes.
  • Review your Scouting America Annual Health and Medical Record with your parent or legal guardian. Discuss your ability to participate in den and pack activities.Home
Bear Habitat
Required
Official adventure page ↗

Built around a one-mile walk practicing Leave No Trace principles. This one happens at the Fall Campout (Oct 23–25) rather than at a den meeting: the hike counts as complete for anyone who attends, and we'll set aside camp time for the rest of the adventure too (route planning, Leave No Trace, fire safety, wildlife spotting). Can't make the campout? There's a make-up hike in November, see below.

  • Prepare for a one-mile walk by gathering the Cub Scout Six Essentials and weather-appropriate clothing and shoes.Home prep
  • Know Before You Go: identify the location of your walk on a map and confirm your 1-mile route.
  • Choose the Right Path: learn about the path and surrounding area you will be walking on.
  • Trash Your Trash: make a plan for what you will do with your personal trash or trash you find along the trail.
  • Leave What You Find: take pictures along your walk, or bring a sketchbook to draw five things you want to remember.
  • Be Careful with Fire: determine the fire danger rating along your path.
  • Respect Wildlife: from a safe distance, identify six signs of mammals, birds, insects, or reptiles as you look up, down, and around you.
  • Be Kind to Other Visitors: identify what you need to do to be kind to others on the path.
  • With your den, pack, or family, go on your 1-mile walk while practicing your Leave No Trace Principles for Kids.At the campout

Bear Paws for Action
Required
Official adventure page ↗

Scouting for Food (Nov 14–15 collection, Nov 21 drop-off) is the service project, so there's no need to line up anything separate. For the nonprofit-research requirement, Storehouse (the drop-off site) is a built-in option since kids get an in-person look when they help with the drop-off. Families are welcome to research a different nonprofit instead, especially if an older troop sibling needs volunteer hours elsewhere.

  • Familiarize yourself with the flag of the United States, including its history, how to raise and lower it, how to properly fold and display it, and flag etiquette.
  • Identify three symbols that represent the United States. Pick your favorite and make a model, work of art, or other craft that depicts the symbol.
  • Learn about the mission of any nonprofit. Find out how it funds its activities and how volunteers are used to help.Try Storehouse
  • Participate in a service project.Scouting for Food, Nov 14–21

Bear Standing Tall
Required*
Official adventure page ↗

Most of this adventure happens at home with a parent: a video, a worksheet, and a conversation about family digital-device rules. The one piece that benefits from a group is the safety-gear demo, a good fit for the December den get-together (cookie decorating at Mark's house, date TBA).

  • With permission from your parent or legal guardian, watch the Protect Yourself Rules video for the Bear rank.Home
  • Complete the Personal Space Bubble worksheet that is part of the Protect Yourself Rules resources.Home
  • With your parent or legal guardian, set up a family policy for digital devices.Home
  • Identify common personal safety gear for your head, eyes, mouth and nose, hands, and feet. List how each item protects you, and demonstrate proper use of safety gear for an activity.Den get-together
Bear Fellowship
Required*
Official adventure page ↗

Almost entirely family-based, which pairs it naturally with Standing Tall this month. Three requirements are conversations and a craft or food item at home, and the act of kindness can happen any time during December.

  • With your parent or legal guardian, talk about your family's faith traditions. Identify three holidays or celebrations that are part of your family's faith traditions, and make a craft, work of art, or food item tied to your favorite one.Home
  • With your family, attend a religious service or other gathering that shows how your family expresses reverence.Home
  • Carry out an act of kindness.Home
  • With your parent or legal guardian, identify a religion or faith different from your own, and determine two things it has in common with your family's beliefs.Home

Marble Madness
Elective
Official adventure page ↗

Indoor-friendly and low-prep: just marbles, string or yarn for the bag, and space to build a racetrack and a maze. Mary Heather is spearheading supplies and craft time for this one, from marble bags to the racetrack build, so she's the person to thank (or ask for help). With this done, the den has its 6 required adventures plus 2 electives, and Bear rank is complete.

  • Make a marble bag.
  • Learn five words that are used when talking about marbles.
  • Play three different marble games.
  • Build a marble racetrack with at least two lanes.
  • Build a marble maze.

Whittling
Elective (bonus)

  • Create two carving projects using a pocketknife.
